# PACA++ Licensing and Commercial Use

## Current legal status — existing code

The repository's root `LICENSE` remains the MIT License. PACA++ v0.3.1 and the code distributed in the repository and container image with that MIT notice were made available under MIT terms.

MIT permits commercial use, modification, distribution, and sale, subject to its notice conditions. The project cannot retroactively withdraw permissions already granted to people who received those copies under MIT. A later change to this repository does not make those earlier MIT copies royalty-bearing.

Accordingly, **no royalty or revenue share is due under the existing MIT grant merely because someone commercially uses a copy they received under MIT**. Keep the MIT notice with those copies and distributions.

## Proposed model for genuinely new components

The project is considering a separate academic and non-commercial license for original PACA++ components created in the future. The proposed terms are in [LICENSE-ACADEMIC-DRAFT.md](LICENSE-ACADEMIC-DRAFT.md).

That file is expressly marked **DRAFT** and is not currently applied to any file or release. It must not be treated as a licence already governing the existing source tree.

Before any new component is released under the proposed terms:

1. Identify it explicitly in the source file header and in the release notes.
2. Confirm that the person or organisation applying the license owns or is authorised to license all relevant copyright in that component.
3. Obtain written licensing permission from every contributor whose work is included, using an appropriate contributor agreement where necessary.
4. Keep MIT-licensed legacy files and third-party dependencies clearly identified; do not silently relicense them.
5. Have the final license, contributor process, and package/release metadata reviewed for legal consistency.

A future release may therefore contain components under different terms. The applicable license must be stated at file and release level; do not assume one license applies to every file.

## Commercial licensing for future covered components

Commercial use of a component that is validly released under the future academic/non-commercial terms would require a separate written agreement signed by the relevant rights holder(s) before commercial use begins.

That agreement may include a licence fee, annual fee, royalty, revenue-share percentage, minimum commitment, or another negotiated payment structure. The amount and calculation method are **not set here** and must be written into the signed agreement. There is no automatic royalty percentage.

For licensing enquiries, contact the repository maintainer through the [project owner's GitHub profile](https://github.com/sanafirdouse2323-glitch). Do not post confidential commercial proposals or personal information in a public issue.

## Open-source terminology

A license that excludes commercial use is not an OSI-approved open-source license. The future model described here is source-available with free academic/non-commercial permission and separate commercial licensing. It should not be advertised as fully open source.

## Important

This document describes the current MIT status and a proposed future licensing direction; it is not legal advice and does not itself create a commercial royalty obligation. Obtain advice from an intellectual-property lawyer before putting a new licensing model into effect.
